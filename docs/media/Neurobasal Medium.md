# Neurobasal Medium

## Quick Facts

| Feature | Information |
|----------|-------------|
| Full Name | Neurobasal Medium |
| Medium Type | Serum-Free Basal Medium |
| Primary Use | Long-term neuronal culture |
| Developed For | Primary neurons and neuronal differentiation |
| Serum Requirement | Serum-free (requires supplements) |
| Typical Supplements | B27, GlutaMAX/L-Glutamine, Neurotrophic Factors |
| Storage | 2-8°C, protected from light |

---

# Overview

Neurobasal Medium is a serum-free basal medium specifically formulated to support the survival, maturation, and long-term maintenance of primary neurons while minimizing glial proliferation. It is considered the standard basal medium for embryonic and neonatal neuronal cultures and is extensively used in neuroscience, neurodevelopment, and neurodegeneration research.

Unlike general-purpose media, Neurobasal is optimized for the metabolic requirements of post-mitotic neurons, making it particularly suitable for neuronal differentiation, synapse formation, and electrophysiological studies.

---

# Key Features

- Optimized specifically for neuronal survival.
- Supports long-term serum-free neuronal culture.
- Minimizes proliferation of non-neuronal cells.
- Promotes neuronal maturation and synapse formation.
- Compatible with electrophysiology and functional assays.
- Frequently used during neuronal maturation of stem cell-derived cultures and organoids.

# Common Applications

- Primary cortical neuron culture
- Primary hippocampal neuron culture
- iPSC-derived neuronal differentiation
- Dopaminergic neuron culture
- Motor neuron culture
- Synaptic biology
- Electrophysiology
- Neurodevelopment studies
- Neurodegenerative disease models
- Brain and cerebral organoids

---

# Advantages

- Gold-standard medium for primary neurons
- Excellent long-term neuronal survival
- Serum-free formulation
- Low glial proliferation
- Supports neuronal maturation
- Highly reproducible

---

# Limitations

- Not suitable for rapidly dividing cells
- Requires specialized supplements
- Limited support for glial cultures
- Higher cost than general-purpose media
