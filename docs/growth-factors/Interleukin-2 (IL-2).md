# Overview

Interleukin-2 (IL-2) is a cytokine that plays a central role in T-cell activation, proliferation, survival, and immune regulation. Secreted primarily by activated CD4⁺ T lymphocytes, IL-2 functions as an essential growth factor for T cells and is widely used in immunology, cancer research, adoptive cell therapy, and cell culture.

In vitro, IL-2 is indispensable for expanding activated T lymphocytes, maintaining natural killer (NK) cells, and supporting immune cell functional assays.

---

# Biological Function

IL-2 regulates several immune processes, including:

* Promotes T-cell proliferation
* Supports T-cell survival
* Enhances NK cell activation
* Maintains regulatory T cells (Tregs)
* Stimulates cytokine production
* Supports cytotoxic lymphocyte function
* Regulates adaptive immune responses

---

# Mechanism of Action

IL-2 binds to the **IL-2 receptor (IL-2R)**, which exists in multiple affinity states depending on receptor subunit composition.

Receptor activation stimulates several intracellular signaling pathways, including:

* JAK1/JAK3-STAT5 pathway
* PI3K-AKT pathway
* MAPK/ERK pathway

These pathways regulate lymphocyte proliferation, survival, metabolism, and differentiation.

# Common Applications

IL-2 is widely used for:

# Advantages

* Essential growth factor for activated T cells
* Supports long-term immune cell culture
* Enhances NK cell proliferation and cytotoxicity
* Widely validated in immunology research
* Critical reagent for adoptive cell therapies
* Compatible with serum-free immune cell media

---

# Limitations

* Activity depends on IL-2 receptor expression
* High concentrations may promote activation-induced cell death (AICD) in some settings
* Can preferentially expand regulatory T cells under specific conditions
* Sensitive to repeated freeze-thaw cycles
* Frequently requires combination with additional cytokines

# Key Takeaways

* IL-2 is the principal cytokine regulating **T-cell proliferation and survival**.
* It signals through the **IL-2 receptor complex**, activating JAK-STAT, PI3K-AKT, and MAPK signaling pathways.
* IL-2 is indispensable for T-cell expansion, NK cell culture, CAR-T manufacturing, and immunotherapy research.
* Most immune cell culture protocols specify IL-2 concentrations in **IU/mL** rather than ng/mL.
* Typical working concentrations range from **10-1,000 IU/mL**, depending on the application.
