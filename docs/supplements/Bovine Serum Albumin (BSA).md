# Overview

Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA) is a highly purified plasma protein widely used in mammalian cell culture as a carrier protein, stabilizer, and protective supplement. Unlike fetal bovine serum (FBS), BSA provides a defined protein source without introducing large amounts of growth factors or other undefined biological components.

BSA improves serum-free and chemically defined culture systems by stabilizing growth factors, transporting hydrophobic molecules, reducing nonspecific adsorption, and protecting cells from environmental stress.

# Biological Role

BSA performs several important functions in cell culture:

* Transports fatty acids, lipids, steroid hormones, vitamins, and metal ions.
* Stabilizes cytokines, enzymes, antibodies, and growth factors.
* Helps maintain osmotic balance.
* Binds potentially toxic compounds and free radicals.
* Reduces nonspecific protein adsorption to laboratory plastics.

Although BSA supports cell viability, it does **not** replace the biological activity of serum and is typically combined with additional supplements or growth factors.

# Common Applications

BSA is routinely used for:

* Serum-free and chemically defined media
* Stem cell culture
* Organoid culture
* Primary cell culture
* Growth factor stabilization
* Cell washing buffers
* Flow cytometry
* Immunocytochemistry
* Drug screening
* Cryopreservation solutions

# Advantages

* Chemically defined protein source
* Stabilizes growth factors and cytokines
* Supports serum-free culture
* Reduces experimental variability
* Improves protein stability
* Compatible with numerous mammalian cell types
* Widely validated and readily available

# Limitations

* Does not replace serum completely
* Provides minimal intrinsic growth factor activity
* Different purification grades have different properties
* Non-cell culture grades may contain contaminants unsuitable for culture applications
