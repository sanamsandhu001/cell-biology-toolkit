# Overview

ITS Supplement is a chemically defined mixture of **Insulin, Transferrin, and Selenium** that replaces several growth-supporting functions normally provided by fetal bovine serum (FBS). It promotes cell survival, metabolism, and proliferation while reducing variability associated with serum-containing media.

ITS is widely used in serum-free and reduced-serum culture systems, particularly for stem cells, hepatocytes, epithelial cells, endothelial cells, and organoid cultures.

# Composition

| Component   | Function                                                           |
| :---------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Insulin     | Stimulates glucose uptake, metabolism, and protein synthesis       |
| Transferrin | Delivers iron required for cell growth and DNA synthesis           |
| Selenium    | Supports antioxidant enzymes and protects against oxidative stress |

> **Note:** Commercial formulations (e.g., ITS-A, ITS-G, ITS-X) may contain additional components or modified concentrations. Always consult the manufacturer's specifications.

# Biological Role

ITS replaces several essential serum functions while maintaining a defined culture environment.

* **Insulin** promotes glucose uptake, amino acid transport, and activation of anabolic signaling pathways.
* **Transferrin** supplies bioavailable iron while minimizing oxidative damage from free iron.
* **Selenium** functions as a cofactor for antioxidant enzymes, supporting cellular redox balance and viability.

Together, these components improve growth and reproducibility while minimizing the variability introduced by serum.

# Common Applications

ITS is commonly used for:

* Serum-free and reduced-serum culture
* Primary hepatocyte maintenance
* Stem cell differentiation
* Mesenchymal stem cell culture
* Endothelial and epithelial cell culture
* Liver and brain organoids
* Tissue engineering
* Developmental biology
* Drug screening

# Advantages

* Chemically defined composition
* Reduces dependence on serum
* Improves experimental reproducibility
* Supports cellular metabolism
* Enhances antioxidant protection
* Compatible with many primary cell types
* Suitable for serum-free media development

# Limitations

* Does not completely replace serum for all cell types
* Additional growth factors are often required
* Performance depends on the specific cell type and protocol
* Commercial formulations are not always interchangeable
