## Overview

MDA-MB-231 is one of the most widely used models of aggressive breast cancer. Unlike MCF-7 cells, which are hormone responsive, MDA-MB-231 cells represent **triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)** and are characterized by high migratory and invasive potential.

The cell line is extensively used to investigate epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), metastasis, tumor progression, and therapeutic resistance.

---

## Origin

- Established from the pleural effusion of a patient with metastatic breast adenocarcinoma.
- Developed at **The University of Texas MD Anderson Hospital**, from which the "MDA" designation is derived.
- Represents a classic model of triple-negative breast cancer.

---

## Unique Biology

MDA-MB-231 cells lack expression of:

- Estrogen Receptor (ER)
- Progesterone Receptor (PR)
- HER2

making them a representative model of **triple-negative breast cancer**.

A defining feature of this cell line is its **mesenchymal-like phenotype**, characterized by:

- High migratory capacity
- Strong invasive behavior
- Cytoskeletal remodeling
- Features associated with epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT)

Because of these properties, MDA-MB-231 cells are widely used to investigate tumor invasion, metastatic mechanisms, extracellular matrix interactions, and cancer cell plasticity.

---

## Culture Considerations

Typical culture conditions include:

- DMEM
- 10% Fetal Bovine Serum
- 37°C
- 5% CO₂

Cells grow as adherent cultures and are generally passaged at **70-90% confluency**.

Migration and invasion assays are highly sensitive to cell density and passage number; therefore, standardized culture conditions are essential for reproducible results.

---

## Major Applications

MDA-MB-231 cells are extensively used for:

- Triple-negative breast cancer research
- Metastasis studies
- Cell migration assays
- Invasion assays
- EMT research
- Drug resistance studies
- Cancer stem cell biology
- Drug discovery
- Gene expression studies

---

## Strengths

- Well-established TNBC model
- Highly migratory and invasive phenotype
- Human-derived cancer cell line
- Extensive scientific literature
- Excellent model for EMT and metastasis research
- Widely used for therapeutic development

---

## Limitations

- Represents only one subtype of breast cancer
- Long-term culture may alter cellular characteristics
- Does not reproduce the complexity of the tumor microenvironment
- Findings should be validated using complementary TNBC models
- Not suitable for studies of hormone-responsive breast cancer
