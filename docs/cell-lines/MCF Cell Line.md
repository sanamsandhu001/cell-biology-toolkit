## Overview

MCF-7 is one of the most widely used human breast cancer cell lines and serves as a standard model for studying hormone-responsive breast cancer. The cell line is particularly valued because it expresses **Estrogen Receptor Alpha (ERα)**, allowing researchers to investigate estrogen signaling, endocrine therapies, and mechanisms of hormone-dependent tumor growth.

Its robust growth and reproducible behavior have made MCF-7 a cornerstone model in cancer biology and pharmaceutical research.

---

## Origin

- Established in 1970 from the pleural effusion of a patient with metastatic breast adenocarcinoma.
- Developed at the **Michigan Cancer Foundation**, from which the name **MCF** is derived.
- Represents an estrogen receptor-positive (ER-positive) breast cancer model.

---

## Unique Biology

The defining feature of MCF-7 cells is their expression of **Estrogen Receptor Alpha (ERα)**.

When estrogen binds ERα, it regulates gene expression and promotes cellular proliferation, making MCF-7 an ideal model for investigating hormone-dependent cancers.

MCF-7 cells are also commonly **Progesterone Receptor (PR) positive**, reflecting functional estrogen signaling pathways.

Compared with more aggressive breast cancer models, MCF-7 cells display relatively low invasiveness, making them particularly useful for studying early-stage and hormone-responsive breast cancer rather than metastatic disease.

For hormone-response experiments, researchers frequently use **charcoal-stripped serum** to minimize endogenous steroid hormones before treatment.

---

## Culture Considerations

Typical culture conditions include:

- DMEM or RPMI-1640
- 10% Fetal Bovine Serum
- 37°C
- 5% CO₂

Cells grow as adherent monolayers and are generally passaged at **70-90% confluency**.

Because hormone-responsive pathways are influenced by serum composition, consistent culture conditions are essential for reproducible endocrine studies.

---

## Major Applications

MCF-7 cells are extensively used for:

- Breast cancer biology
- Estrogen receptor signaling
- Endocrine therapy research
- Drug screening
- Hormone-response studies
- Cancer metabolism
- Drug resistance research
- Gene expression studies

---

## Strengths

- Human-derived breast cancer model
- ER-positive and hormone responsive
- Easy to culture and maintain
- Extensive scientific literature
- Highly reproducible experimental system
- Standard model for endocrine therapy research

---

## Limitations

- Represents only one molecular subtype of breast cancer
- Relatively low invasive potential
- Long-term culture may alter cellular characteristics
- Lacks the complexity of the tumor microenvironment
- Findings should be validated using additional breast cancer models
