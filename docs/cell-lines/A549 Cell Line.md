## Overview

A549 is one of the most widely used human lung cancer cell lines. Derived from a lung adenocarcinoma, it retains several characteristics of alveolar type II epithelial cells, making it a valuable model for both cancer and pulmonary research.

Its versatility has established A549 as a standard model for investigating lung cancer, respiratory infections, environmental toxicology, oxidative stress, and drug discovery.

---

## Origin

- Established in 1972 from a human lung adenocarcinoma.
- Derived from alveolar epithelial tissue.
- One of the most extensively characterized lung epithelial cell models.

---

## Unique Biology

A549 cells exhibit several features associated with **alveolar type II epithelial cells**, including the ability to produce components of pulmonary surfactant.

They are widely used to investigate:

- Lung epithelial biology
- Pulmonary surfactant function
- Respiratory infections
- Air pollution toxicity
- Cigarette smoke exposure
- Nanoparticle toxicity

A549 cells also harbor an activating **KRAS mutation**, making them an important model for studies of lung adenocarcinoma signaling and targeted therapies.

Although they retain many pulmonary epithelial characteristics, A549 cells are derived from a malignant tumor and should not be considered representative of normal lung tissue.

---

## Culture Considerations

Typical culture conditions include:

- F-12K or DMEM
- 10% Fetal Bovine Serum
- 37°C
- 5% CO₂

Cells grow as adherent monolayers and are generally passaged at **70-90% confluency**.

For toxicology and infection studies, consistent cell density and standardized exposure conditions are important for reproducible results.

---

## Major Applications

A549 cells are extensively used for:

- Lung cancer biology
- Respiratory epithelial biology
- Drug screening
- Respiratory infection research
- Toxicology
- Air pollution studies
- Nanotoxicology
- Oxidative stress research
- Cancer metabolism
- Gene expression studies

---

## Strengths

- Human-derived lung epithelial model
- Easy to culture and maintain
- Suitable for both cancer and respiratory research
- Extensive scientific literature
- Widely used for toxicology and infection studies
- Well characterized genetically

---

## Limitations

- Derived from lung adenocarcinoma rather than normal lung tissue
- Represents only one subtype of lung cancer
- Contains an activating KRAS mutation that influences cellular behavior
- Lacks the complex architecture of the respiratory tract
- Findings should be validated using primary airway epithelial cells or more advanced lung models
