## Overview

C8-D1A is a widely used immortalized mouse astrocyte cell line that provides a reproducible model for investigating astrocyte function in the central nervous system. It is commonly employed to study neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, neuron-glia communication, and the role of astrocytes in neurological disorders.

Because primary astrocytes are difficult to isolate and exhibit considerable biological variability, C8-D1A offers a practical alternative for routine laboratory studies.

---

## Origin

- Derived from mouse cerebellar astrocytes.
- Developed as an immortalized astrocyte model for neuroscience research.
- Serves as a renewable alternative to primary astrocyte cultures.

---

## Unique Biology

C8-D1A cells reproduce many functional properties of astrocytes, including regulation of neuronal homeostasis and responses to inflammatory stimuli.

They are frequently used to investigate:

- Astrocyte activation
- Reactive astrogliosis
- Cytokine signaling
- Oxidative stress
- Neuron-glia communication

C8-D1A cells also express several astrocytic markers, including **GFAP**, **S100β**, **GLAST**, and **GLT-1**, although expression may vary with culture conditions and activation state.

While they model many astrocytic functions, C8-D1A cells cannot fully reproduce the complexity and regional diversity of primary astrocytes within the brain.

---

## Culture Considerations

Typical culture conditions include:

- DMEM
- 10% Fetal Bovine Serum
- 37°C
- 5% CO₂

Cells grow as adherent monolayers and are generally passaged at **70-90% confluency**.

Experimental responses may vary with passage number and inflammatory stimulation, making standardized culture conditions important for reproducibility.

---

## Major Applications

C8-D1A cells are extensively used for:

- Astrocyte biology
- Neuroinflammation
- Reactive astrogliosis
- Oxidative stress research
- Neuron-glia interaction studies
- Alzheimer's disease research
- Parkinson's disease research
- Stroke and ischemia models
- Drug screening
- Neuroprotection studies

---

## Strengths

- Well-established astrocyte model
- Easy to culture and maintain
- Reproducible experimental responses
- Suitable for glial biology research
- Cost-effective alternative to primary astrocytes
- Extensive neuroscience literature

---

## Limitations

- Mouse-derived rather than human
- Immortalized cell line that differs from primary astrocytes
- Does not reproduce the cellular complexity of the CNS
- Limited representation of astrocyte heterogeneity
- Findings should be validated using primary astrocytes or in vivo models when appropriate
