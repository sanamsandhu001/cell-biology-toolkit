## Overview

THP-1 is one of the most widely used human monocytic cell lines in immunology research. Derived from the peripheral blood of a patient with acute monocytic leukemia, THP-1 cells provide a robust model for studying innate immune responses, inflammatory signaling, and host-pathogen interactions.

A defining characteristic of THP-1 cells is their ability to differentiate into macrophage-like cells following chemical stimulation, allowing researchers to investigate both circulating monocytes and tissue macrophages within a single experimental system.

---

## Origin

- Established from the peripheral blood of a patient with acute monocytic leukemia.
- Developed as a continuously proliferating human monocyte model.
- Widely used in immunology, inflammation, infection biology, and functional genomics.

---

## Unique Biology

The hallmark feature of THP-1 cells is their ability to differentiate into macrophage-like cells following stimulation with **Phorbol 12-Myristate 13-Acetate (PMA)**. Differentiated cells become adherent, increase in size, exhibit macrophage-like morphology, and display enhanced phagocytic activity, making THP-1 an excellent model for macrophage biology.

THP-1 cells express numerous **pattern-recognition receptors**, including Toll-like receptors (TLRs) and NOD-like receptors (NLRs), enabling detailed investigation of innate immune signaling, inflammatory pathways, and host-pathogen interactions.

Activated THP-1 cells produce inflammatory cytokines such as **TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-8**, making them one of the most widely used models for studying cytokine regulation and inflammatory diseases.

THP-1 cells are also extensively employed to investigate activation of the **NLRP3 inflammasome**, a critical regulator of innate immunity involved in infection, chronic inflammation, metabolic disorders, and autoimmune disease.

## Culture Considerations

Typical culture conditions include:

- RPMI-1640 Medium
- 10% Fetal Bovine Serum
- 37°C
- 5% CO₂

THP-1 cells grow as suspension cultures and are maintained by regular dilution rather than enzymatic passaging. Cell density should generally be maintained between **2 × 10⁵ and 8 × 10⁵ cells/mL** to ensure optimal growth and reproducible immune responses. PMA-differentiated cells become adherent and should be handled accordingly during downstream experiments.

## Major Applications

THP-1 cells are extensively used for:

- Monocyte biology
- Macrophage differentiation
- Innate immune signaling
- Cytokine biology
- Inflammasome research
- Host-pathogen interactions
- Atherosclerosis research
- Cancer immunology
- Drug discovery

---

## Strengths

- Well-established human monocyte model
- Differentiates into macrophage-like cells
- Excellent model for innate immune signaling
- Highly reproducible experimental responses
- Extensive scientific literature support

---

## Limitations

- Derived from leukemic rather than normal primary monocytes
- PMA-induced macrophages do not fully replicate tissue macrophages
- Simplified immune system lacking interactions with other cell types
- Certain signaling responses differ from primary human immune cells
