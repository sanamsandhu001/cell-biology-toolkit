## Overview

BV2 is one of the most widely used immortalized microglial cell lines in neuroscience research. As a surrogate for primary microglia, BV2 cells provide a convenient and reproducible model for studying neuroinflammation, innate immune responses, cytokine production, and neurodegenerative disease mechanisms.

Because primary microglia are difficult to isolate and maintain, BV2 has become a standard experimental model for investigating interactions between the immune system and the central nervous system.

---

## Origin

- Generated through immortalization of murine microglial cells.
- Developed as an alternative to primary microglial cultures.
- Retains many functional characteristics of activated microglia while allowing continuous propagation.

---

## Unique Biology

BV2 cells model many of the innate immune functions performed by microglia within the central nervous system.

Upon stimulation, BV2 cells undergo activation characterized by:

- Production of inflammatory cytokines
- Increased phagocytic activity
- Morphological changes
- Altered immune gene expression

Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is the most commonly used activator of BV2 cells, although other inflammatory stimuli such as interferon-γ, amyloid-β, and α-synuclein are also widely employed depending on the experimental model.

While BV2 cells reproduce many aspects of microglial activation, they should not be considered fully equivalent to primary microglia.

---

## Culture Considerations

Typical culture conditions include:

- DMEM
- 10% Fetal Bovine Serum
- 37°C
- 5% CO₂

BV2 cells grow as adherent cultures and are generally passaged at **70-80% confluency**.

Because inflammatory responses are influenced by passage number, cell density, and activation protocol, maintaining consistent experimental conditions is essential for reproducible results.

---

## Major Applications

BV2 cells are extensively used for:

- Neuroinflammation research
- Microglial activation studies
- Innate immune signaling
- Cytokine biology
- Phagocytosis assays
- Alzheimer's disease research
- Parkinson's disease research
- Stroke and ischemia models
- Neurotoxicity studies
- Drug screening

---

## Strengths

- Widely established microglial model
- Easy to culture and maintain
- Highly reproducible
- Suitable for large-scale experiments
- Extensive neuroscience literature
- Useful for studying neuroimmune signaling

---

## Limitations

- Mouse-derived rather than human
- Immortalized cell line that differs from primary microglia
- Simplified model lacking interactions with other CNS cell types
- Activation responses may differ from those observed in vivo
- Findings should be validated using primary microglia or animal models when appropriate
